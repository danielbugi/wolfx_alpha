# Collector activation-timing safety review (2026-10-08)

Requested by the owner after Phase 1. **No production change was made and none is required for this review.** One scheduler adjustment and one verification gap were found and fixed
on the lab branch, producing a **new candidate SHA** (named in the review message; the approved production SHA `66b23b9dcbfa` is untouched and still what production runs).

## 1. The evidence that triggered the review
The first full pipeline cycle on the new pin (session 2026-10-07) ended at **00:13:43Z = 03:13:43 Asia/Jerusalem**, because the earnings-calendar updater re-fetched everything (62,955 rows; it
re-checks every `REFRESH_AFTER_DAYS = 14` days; the file is unchanged by the candidate). Normal nights end about 02:25. The collector's first fire was 03:15: **77 seconds** after the slowest observed night.

## 2. Findings, in the owner's order
| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Schedule and DST | Both fires are `OnCalendar=... Asia/Jerusalem` local times on the calendar day after a session, like every other timer. The pipeline timers are also Jerusalem-local, so their offset to the fires never changes. The session each fire collects is chosen from the **US calendar in New York time** (`latest_completed` = close + 2 h settle), not from the Jerusalem clock: in every regime (both on DST, neither, and the two mismatch windows) a first fire is at 21:00-22:00 New York time on the evening of the session itself. Weekend and Monday fires re-resolve Friday and are idempotent re-checks. Pinned by `test_schedule_timing_pure.py`. |
| 2 | Move the first attempt to 04:00? | **Yes.** 04:00 leaves 46 minutes after the slowest observed night, follows the 03:30 ledger evaluator, and 08:15 stays as the recovery attempt (after the 05:00 price safety net). The validator now treats a first fire before 03:45 (worst observed finish 03:15 + 30 min margin) as invalid, so the old 03:15 is rejected by the design check. Both fires are still before the decision deadline: `required_grace_days() == 1`, worst case about 18 hours of slack. |
| 3 | Does it check completion and session identity, not just the clock? | Mostly yes, with **one gap, now fixed.** Observe requires the session's **own price bar to be the newest stored bar** (a later bar makes the session MISSED, never re-attributed); verify re-reads the written snapshots and their stamps; the sector-history step reads the actual polls; labels/RS rows are re-counted against the universe. The gap: the wrapper did **not** pass `--with-capture-check`, so a session whose screener/capture had failed could still end `COMPLETE`. Fixed (see section 3). |
| 4 | Late / failed / partial pipeline | Proven with the real writers and a simulated clock (`test_schedule_dependencies_db.py`): a late pipeline gives INCOMPLETE (exit 2) at 04:00 and COMPLETE at 08:15 with **no duplicate row** in any table and an idempotent third run; a pipeline that never loaded the session gives INCOMPLETE at both fires and writes nothing; a capture run left `running` or `failed` is never COMPLETE; once a later bar is newest the earlier session is MISSED (exit 4) and nothing is stamped under it; the clock alone (0 to 8 hours late) never produces COMPLETE. |
| 5 | 4-day fundamentals window; can a session be skipped silently? | The window is appropriate: the sector-history **freshness** rule is 30 days, the **cadence monitor** is 4 days (`MAX_REFRESH_GAP_DAYS`); the observed run-date gaps were 1-3 days, once 3 (09-28 to 10-01). A gap of 5 days or more makes the check fail (INCOMPLETE, then MISSED after the deadline). No silent skip: every weekday session gets two attempts before its deadline; weekend/Monday fires re-check Friday; any failure is exit 2 or 4. **Residual (not a data risk):** the alert unit is journald-only (`/bin/true`), so INCOMPLETE/MISSED is visible as a failed unit, in the log file and in the JSON report, but nothing is *pushed*; and a session whose both fires failed is no longer re-examined by the timer once the next session completes (it stays INCOMPLETE; the research status shows the gap and it is never backfilled). The owner should decide the alert transport before relying on it unattended. |
| 6 | MCHB `numeric field overflow` | **Isolated; does not affect forward collection.** The overflow is in `technical_indicators` (`price_position` / `channel_width_pct` are `numeric(6,2)`), caused by `MCHB`'s history before a 2025-09-02 price discontinuity (closes up to 39,103 against about 14.6 today). MCHB has **zero** indicator rows and no ledger signal, so the screener never sees it as a candidate. The collector reads `stock_prices` (not `technical_indicators`), the discontinuity is recorded in `price_discontinuities` and is outside every 5/20/60-session window, and MCHB's current prices are normal, so the market/sector/relative-strength rows are unaffected. 17 other symbols also have stale indicators (AAC, ADIG, APMD, ATTO, AUXX, BLSM, BRVE, CNL, HOS, JMKE, LTGO, LYNX, NXH, QVCG, REF, VOGX, XLAB); that is a pre-existing coverage gap in the screener's input, not a forward-collection correctness issue. |

## 3. What changed on the lab branch (the smallest change)
* `deploy/vps/donchian-forward-collection.timer`: first fire 03:15 -> **04:00**; 08:15 unchanged.
* `contract.SCHEDULER_DESIGN`: `fires_local ("04:00","08:15")`, `pipeline_worst_finish_local "03:15"` (observed), command now includes `--with-capture-check`.
* `deploy/vps/run_forward_collection.sh`: passes `--with-capture-check`.
* `steps.capture_verify`: **boundary-aware**: it applies only for strategies whose latest `research_capture_activation` row effective on or before the session says `enabled` (the screener hook's own rule). A session before the boundary is not a failure; a session on or after it without a complete run for every enabled strategy is.
* `activation.py`: the dormancy check requires the capture flag in the wrapper; a new architecture check `scheduler_command_verifies_capture`.
* Tests: `test_schedule_timing_pure.py` (11), `test_schedule_dependencies_db.py` (7; two fail on the old capture check), updated contract test, runbook text and manifest.
